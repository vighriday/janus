# Project Review: Returns & Reverse-Logistics Redesign (Phase 1)

**Prepared by:** Dolores Tran, Ops Excellence Lead
**Sponsor:** Marcus Eddington, VP Warehouse Operations
**Review window:** Mar 2022 – Aug 2022

## Why we did this
Returns at the Hartfield DC were a mess. Inbound returns sat on the receiving dock an average of 6.2 days before disposition, tying up roughly 1,100 sq ft of floor space and generating constant noise from the customer-service team. We were eating the storage cost and, worse, ~14% of returnable inventory was aging past the resale window before anyone graded it.

## What we changed
We rebuilt the flow end to end, not the software, the physical process.

1. **Single intake lane.** Returns now hit a dedicated 3-station grading bench instead of being mixed into general receiving. Carlos Min's team owns it.
2. **48-hour disposition SLA.** Every returned unit gets graded (A/B/C/scrap) within two business days. We laminated a one-page decision tree so seasonal staff don't escalate every judgment call.
3. **Batched restock windows.** A-grade goes back to pickable stock twice daily (10:00 and 15:00) instead of trickling in ad hoc.

## Results so far
- Dock dwell time: **6.2 days → 1.8 days** (–71%).
- Resale-window misses: **14% → 4%**.
- Reclaimed ~700 sq ft of dock space, now used for cross-dock staging.
- Grading throughput up ~40% per labor hour, mostly from killing the back-and-forth with CS.

## What didn't go well
- The decision tree was too vague on cosmetic B/C calls. We saw inconsistent grading between the early and late shifts (one shift was scrapping recoverable units). Fixed in week 6 with photo reference cards.
- We underestimated training time. Budgeted 2 hours per associate; real number was closer to 5.

## Decision
**Approved to roll Phase 2 to the Pell City DC in Q1 2023.** Marcus signed off Sept 12. Phase 2 owner: Carlos Min. No new headcount; we fund it from the reclaimed throughput. Revisit the grading rubric quarterly with Returns CS.
